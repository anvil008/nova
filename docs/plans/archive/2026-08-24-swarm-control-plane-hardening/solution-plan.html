Recommendation

Make the existing fleet boundaries enforceable, versioned, and independently provable

The coding fleet already describes roles, renderers, foreign-harness routing, and bounded repair behavior, but critical safeguards are distributed between prose, renderer settings, and early run-plane code. The recommended design is a small shared control-plane contract layer plus thin Codex, Claude, and Antigravity adapters. Runtime admission computes a narrowing authority intersection; every dispatch carries an exact route; every return carries a validated evidence envelope; and a generation-fenced supervisor owns cancellation, aggregate budgets, and file/symbol leases.

Delivery shape. First freeze schemas and conformance fixtures; then enforce authority/selection/route/result behavior, add lifecycle fencing and structured supervisor tools, contain Factory mutation, implement the bounded independent verification loop, and only then perform a separately authorized backup-first LXC sync.

Largest risk: harness adapters can appear aligned while runtime permissions or late process output still diverge. Shared negative-path conformance and current-state evidence—not rendered text alone—are therefore release gates. Immediate next decision: accept the versioned contracts and the default of two total verification passes so Stage 2 can decompose this design without reopening core semantics.

Problem and opportunity

Affected actors

  • Repository owners need bounded autonomous progress without a stale child overwriting newer work.
  • Fleet operators need exact model routing, cancellation, budgets, evidence, rollback, and observable terminal reasons.
  • Workflow units need authority to select the right technical/domain leaf without bypass by the orchestrator.
  • Independent reviewers need read-only evidence that is not supplied solely by the writer.

Current failure modes

  • Role prose and rendered tool lists can drift from effective runtime authority.
  • Provider/model intent is not yet a complete, versioned per-dispatch envelope.
  • Cancellation is job-local; generations, descendant draining, and late-result rejection are absent.
  • Path overlap checks do not cover symbol-level ownership or goal-wide budget reservations.
  • Workflow output remains mostly narrative, so completion can be asserted without required machine evidence.

Why now: the dirty worktree already contains a new runplane/ and generated cross-harness projections. This is the least costly point to define stable contracts before ad hoc behavior becomes compatibility debt. The opportunity is not to add another agent framework; it is to turn existing ownership intent into one small, testable enforcement seam.

Goals, non-goals, and success

Goals

  • Authority can only narrow from parent to child and matches rendered/runtime tools.
  • Workflow selection, exact routes, results, evidence, generations, budgets, and ownership are explicit contracts.
  • Claude and Antigravity orchestrators get lifecycle capability without arbitrary shell.
  • Factory and target-project mutation stay disjoint and recoverable.
  • Independent verification is bounded, deterministic, and scorer-free.

Non-goals

  • Replacing native harnesses or making the supervisor a new orchestrator.
  • Allowing the top-level orchestrator to invoke leaf specialists directly.
  • Giving any read-only role a generic command tunnel or project-write capability.
  • Using benchmark gold, hidden tests, scores, history, issue-web data, or paid evaluation.
  • Deploying to LXC 135 during planning.

Success measures

  • Every dispatch and return validates against a versioned schema.
  • Cross-harness negative cases reject identically.
  • Cancelled/stale generations cannot mutate or satisfy completion.
  • Over-budget and ownership conflicts resolve deterministically.
  • One backup-first rollback drill proves byte/settings recovery before LXC acceptance.
Explicit failure conditions: any authority expansion, unversioned dispatch/result, missing independent proof presented as success, arbitrary-shell lifecycle access, Factory target-project write, stale-generation mutation, scorer use, settings drift, or inability to restore an LXC preimage blocks acceptance.

Requirements and acceptance criteria

All nine requirements are confirmed and must. Their source is the user’s control-plane hardening brief as reconciled by the Coding Orchestrator.

REQ-001 · Authority

Inherited, narrowed authority intersection

Statement: runtime and rendering must compute the effective grant as the intersection of parent grant, canonical role ceiling, requested narrowing, and observed harness capability; no edge may expand authority.

Actor/value: operators and repository owners receive one explainable permission boundary across harnesses.

Acceptance: versioned authority envelope and denial reasons; renderer/runtime parity; distinct read/write verification authority; cross-harness allow/deny fixtures prove expansion rejection.

Constraints: orchestrator remains orchestration-only; ordinary project writes remain Executor-only; Factory writes only its owned surfaces.

REQ-002 · Selection

Workflow-owned specialist refinement

Statement: the orchestrator proposes a workflow plus technical/domain lenses; the selected workflow may refine them with recorded reasons and alone invokes leaf specialists. Remove the current zero-pool visibility restriction without granting leaf invocation.

Actor/value: workflows can respond to evidence while the peer-layer boundary stays enforceable.

Acceptance: separate proposable and invocable role sets; a selection record captures proposal, refinements, reasons, and leaf owner; direct orchestrator-to-leaf attempts fail in all harnesses.

Dependencies: REQ-001 authority and REQ-004 route validation.

REQ-003 · Supervisor tool

Narrow lifecycle capability, never unrestricted shell

Statement: Claude and Antigravity orchestrator projections expose typed supervisor lifecycle operations only; they must not gain Bash, run_command, arbitrary process execution, arbitrary URL, or arbitrary filesystem access.

Actor/value: foreign harness orchestrators can monitor/resume/cancel work safely.

Acceptance: allowlisted lifecycle operations; authenticated loopback binding; schema validation; renderer and live adapter tests prove shell absence and tool scope.

Constraints: workflows/leaves do not receive the lifecycle tool; Codex keeps its native orchestration surface.

REQ-004 · Route

Exact versioned route on every dispatch

Statement: every workflow and child dispatch carries exact provider, family, model, and effort plus identity/context. Children inherit by default; authorized workflows may refine within discovered allowlists and budgets; ambiguity or absence rejects fail-closed.

Actor/value: users get the model they requested without silent substitution.

Acceptance: anvil.route/v1 validates at native and foreign admission; route/digest appears in result evidence; unavailable/ambiguous/refined cases have deterministic outcomes.

Dependencies: live capability discovery and REQ-007 budget reservation.

REQ-005 · Result

Machine-validated workflow result and evidence

Statement: every workflow return uses a versioned envelope enumerating identity, route, authority, repository state, artifacts, checks, mutations, findings, decisions, budget use, child evidence, missing proof, and truthful disposition.

Actor/value: the orchestrator can reconcile observed evidence rather than accept prose.

Acceptance: schema and semantic validator; required evidence contract checked; only orchestrator declares overall completion; unproven claims become uncertain, never success.

Dependencies: REQ-001, REQ-004, and generation validity from REQ-007.

REQ-006 · Factory

Transactional, catalog-confined Factory ownership

Statement: Factory may mutate only the canonical technical/domain catalog, generated projections, and installer-owned global harness state; it never edits the target project. Every change is transactional with preimages, validation, rollback, and cold reload.

Actor/value: missing specialists can be added without corrupting either a repository task or installed fleet.

Acceptance: explicit path manifest; target-project denial; fault-injection rollback; exact metadata restoration; post-install fresh-session/cold-reload proof; Factory returns control and never redispatches.

Dependencies: REQ-001 and REQ-005 evidence.

REQ-007 · Lifecycle safety

Generation, cancellation, budget, and ownership fencing

Statement: supervise a goal/dispatch tree with monotonic generation IDs, cancellation fence/cascade/drain, stale/late rejection, aggregate budget reservation/debit, and canonical file/symbol leases.

Actor/value: concurrent agents cannot overspend, collide silently, or let obsolete work win.

Acceptance: only current generations mutate or satisfy results; descendants drain after cancellation; late events are audit-only; collisions and over-budget admissions resolve deterministically under concurrent tests.

Constraints: path traversal/symlink escape rejected; ledger/registry survives supervisor restart.

REQ-008 · Conformance

Benchmark-independent adapters and safe LXC sync

Statement: one shared suite must verify native and foreign adapter behavior independently of benchmarks, with correlated observability and a backup/checksum/settings/atomic-sync/rollback procedure for LXC 135.

Actor/value: operators can distinguish control-plane correctness from benchmark performance.

Acceptance: provider-free fixtures run across Codex, Claude, and Antigravity seams; no score/gold dependency; sync gate preserves exact user constraints and proves restoration after induced failure.

Constraints: deployment and service operations require separate authority.

REQ-009 · Verification loop

Bounded independent verify/repair/reverify loop

Statement: the orchestrator owns plan/execute → independent verify/evaluate → reconcile → targeted repair → reverify, with configurable conservative budget and deterministic terminal outcomes.

Actor/value: users receive bounded assurance without an autonomous or scorer-driven loop.

Acceptance: default two total passes (initial independent verification plus at most one targeted repair/reverify); terminal outcome is exactly accepted, exhausted, cancelled, blocked, or no-progress; unproven evidence remains uncertain.

Constraints: one benchmark attempt, one final patch, no scorer between cycles, no gold/hidden/history/issue-web data.

Proposed solution

Create a shared control-plane kernel owned by the Swarm repository. It does not execute coding work; it validates the graph edges around that work. Native dispatch and foreign run-plane dispatch both consume the same immutable envelopes and emit the same events/results.

1. Authority intersection

effective = parentGrant
          ∩ canonicalRoleCeiling
          ∩ requestedNarrowing
          ∩ observedHarnessCapability

Each dimension—lane, tools, filesystem scopes, network/process rights, role targets, model routes, budget, and evidence obligations—is deny-by-default. Missing or incomparable dimensions reject. The result records inputs, effective grant, and denial reasons. Rendering translates the ceiling; admission rechecks it.

Verification separation: Executor may write the target project; Factory may write only fleet-owned catalog/projection/install surfaces; Research, Planner, Code Review, Debugger, Coding Evaluation, and independent test lenses remain read-only; the Coding Orchestrator remains orchestration-only.

2. Proposal versus invocation

Replace the zero-pool validation rule with two explicit concepts:

  • proposableRoleKinds/proposableSpecialistPools: metadata an orchestrator may recommend.
  • invocableRoleKinds/invocableSpecialistPools: runtime edges a role may start.

The orchestrator’s invocable set contains exactly the seven workflow roles. A workflow records why it retained, dropped, or added a proposed technical/domain lens and is the sole caller of a leaf. Renderers expose proposals as context without expanding tool targets.

3. Exact route and capability admission

Attach anvil.route/v1 to every workflow and child dispatch. The route names provider, family, exact model, effort, canonical role/digest, harnesses, authority ref, generation, ownership, budget slice, and evidence contract. Children inherit all route fields unless the owning workflow records an allowlisted refinement reason.

Capability discovery resolves aliases before dispatch. Missing, ambiguous, conflicting, stale, or unavailable exact capability produces a typed rejection; no provider, family, model, or effort substitution is permitted.

4. Truthful evidence reconciliation

A workflow returns anvil.workflow-result/v1, validated structurally and semantically against its dispatch. Artifact/check hashes, repository snapshots, authority use, budget use, child-result digests, and missing proof are explicit. A writer’s tests are useful execution evidence but never the sole independent completion proof.

Only the Coding Orchestrator reconciles the return with current state and declares the overall outcome. A stale generation, changed repository, missing required check, failed check, or unverifiable claim yields uncertain, partial, blocked, failed, or cancelled—not complete.

5. Generation-fenced supervision

Persist a goal tree keyed by immutable goalId, monotonic generationId, dispatchId, and parentDispatchId. Admission atomically reserves aggregate budget and path/symbol leases. Current-generation checks occur before spawn, before each mutation-capable operation, and before result acceptance.

Cancellation writes a durable fence first, stops admission, cascades to descendants, requests graceful termination, escalates within policy, drains processes/events, releases unused reservations, and closes leases. Late events remain audit records tagged rejected_stale and cannot mutate state or satisfy evidence.

6. Transactional Factory

Factory receives an explicit allowlist of canonical catalog entries, generated projections, and installer-owned global definitions. It captures byte and metadata preimages, stages generation, validates every projection, atomically promotes the set, performs an authorized install, and proves behavior in a fresh session/cold reload.

Any validation/install/reload failure restores all preimages and reports the transaction evidence. Target-repository roots are always denied. Factory returns the definition to the orchestrator and never resumes or dispatches another workflow itself.

Bounded independent verification policy

  1. The orchestrator selects a planning/execution workflow and owns its durable goal.
  2. Executor performs the authorized mutation and may run deterministic checks.
  3. A separate read-only Code Review/Coding Evaluation/testing authority verifies the current state and result contract.
  4. The orchestrator reconciles findings. If accepted, stop. If repairable and budget remains, issue one bounded repair brief to Executor.
  5. Run one independent reverify. Then stop with a deterministic terminal outcome.
Default: maxVerificationPasses = 2 total—initial independent verification plus at most one targeted repair/reverify. Trusted configuration may lower it to one; any supported increase must remain under a reviewed hard cap and aggregate budget. A repeated repository/evidence digest triggers no-progress. Uncertain proof can never become accepted.

Interfaces, data, and operator surfaces

No end-user UI is required. The operator surface is typed CLI/tool output and the existing Nexus Plans projection. All envelopes are JSON-compatible, versioned, strict about unknown fields for admission, and preserved as digest-addressed evidence with bounded retention.

anvil.authority/v1

Required fields: apiVersion, grantId, parentGrantId, roleId, roleDigest, lane, capabilityMode, tool allow/deny sets, filesystem read/write scopes, network/process rights, invocable role kinds/IDs, route constraints, budget ceiling, evidence obligations, runtime-observed capabilities, effectiveGrant, denial reasons, and digest.

Filesystem write scopes distinguish target-project paths, Factory catalog/projection/install paths, and no-write roles. Empty means none, never unlimited.

anvil.selection/v1

Required fields: selectionId, goal/generation/dispatch IDs, proposed workflow ID, proposed technical/domain lens IDs with reasons, workflow refinement records (add/remove/retain, evidence refs, reason), selected leaf IDs, workflow owner, catalog/role digests, timestamps, and digest.

Semantic validation rejects leaf invocation whose parent is not the recorded workflow.

anvil.route/v1

Required fields: apiVersion, routeId, dispatchId, parentDispatchId, goalId, generationId, source/target harness, exact provider, family, model, effort, canonical role ID/digest, selection ID, authority ID/digest, repository root/snapshot, file/symbol ownership claims, budget reservation, evidence contract, capability snapshot digest, refinement reason/parent route, onUnavailable=reject, issued/expiry times, and digest.

Inheritance copies the exact tuple. Refinement requires the workflow’s authority, discovered exact capability, budget fit, and an auditable reason. Omission, ambiguous alias, downgrade, or stale discovery rejects.

anvil.workflow-result/v1

Required fields: API version; result/goal/generation/dispatch/parent IDs; canonical role/lane; route, role, selection, authority, and repository snapshot digests; status and truthful completionClaim; summary; artifacts with path/kind/digest; checks with ID/type/status/exit code/timing/output digest; mutations with path/symbol and pre/post digest; findings, decisions, open questions, assumptions, and errors; child result/evidence digests; budget reserved/consumed/released; cancellation/stale markers; required and missing evidence; suggested next action; started/finished timestamps; envelope digest.

completionClaim is complete, partial, blocked, failed, cancelled, or uncertain. Workflow completion is never overall goal completion. Unknown current state or absent proof must be uncertain.

anvil.supervisor-lifecycle/v1

Operations: health, capabilities, start, list, status, events, send, resume, cancel, and evidence. Requests accept typed IDs, cursors, bounded request-file payloads, or stdin content only.

The adapter is fixed to the authenticated loopback supervisor and cannot accept executable names, arbitrary argv, arbitrary URL, or arbitrary path. Claude/Antigravity projections allowlist this tool only for the orchestrator; existing shell denial remains.

Ledger and registry records

Budget dimensions: wall time, turns/messages, tool calls, input/output tokens when observable, child count/concurrency, and any explicitly authorized cost ceiling. Parent reservations cover all descendants; atomic reserve/debit/release prevents oversubscription. Unknown usage is conservative, never treated as zero.

Ownership: canonical repository-relative path leases and language-qualified symbol leases such as go://runplane.Supervisor.Cancel. Symlink/path escape rejects. Writer conflicts use exact/prefix path overlap plus identical/containing symbol scope; the earlier admitted current-generation lease wins and the newer dispatch deterministically rejects.

Lifecycle and retention

Schema readers support explicitly declared versions; incompatible major versions reject. Goal state, admission/cancellation/result events, envelope digests, and small bounded outputs survive restart. Raw prompts, secrets, auth tokens, and unrestricted command output are not telemetry. Retention is configurable and deletion is an operator action outside this implementation plan.

Accessibility and responsiveness

No new product interface is proposed. If lifecycle state is later projected in Nexus, statuses must have text labels in addition to color, keyboard-accessible details, narrow-screen tables/cards, clear empty/loading/error/cancelled states, and downloadable machine evidence. This Stage 1 report itself supplies those baseline semantics.

Quality, security, and operations

Security and least privilege

  • Authenticate the loopback supervisor; never print bearer tokens.
  • Bind lifecycle tools to fixed operations and schemas, not shell wrappers.
  • Canonicalize repository roots/paths; reject traversal and symlink escape.
  • Verify role/catalog/projection digests at admission and return.
  • Deny write/network/process dimensions absent from the authority intersection.

Performance and availability

  • Admission/registry operations are bounded and atomic; avoid global scans per event.
  • Persist small state changes before process launch or cancellation effects.
  • Backpressure at configured concurrency and aggregate budgets; no unbounded queues.
  • Supervisor restart reconciliation fences unknown processes/results until proven current.

Observability

  • Correlate every event by goal, generation, dispatch, parent, role, route digest, and adapter.
  • Measure admissions/rejections by reason, active descendants, budget reserved/used, lease conflicts, cancel latency/drain, stale events, result-validation failure, repair count, and terminal outcome.
  • Expose evidence digests and redacted summaries; omit prompts, credentials, and sensitive output.

Backup, rollout, and recovery

  • Factory transactions capture exact bytes, mode, owner/group, timestamps, and symlink targets.
  • LXC sync backs up adapter plus all eight role projections with a settings/checksum manifest.
  • Stage to temporary paths, validate, atomically promote, cold reload only when separately authorized, then run offline conformance.
  • On any failure, restore preimages and prove hashes/settings match before leaving the gate.
LXC preservation gate. Before any future sync, independently verify and preserve exactly google/gemini-3.7-flash, THINKING_BUDGET=65535, and t.message_limit = 100. Prior file hashes or byte matches do not replace a fresh Stage 3 snapshot. No service restart, benchmark, model call, or port 8888 access is authorized by this plan.

Decisions and trade-offs

DEC-001 · Accepted

Authority is a versioned intersection

Addresses: REQ-001.

Options: prose-only ceiling; broad enum equality; composable intersection.

Evidence/assumption: current catalog has broad modes and run-plane checks equality. Rationale: intersection preserves inheritance and explains denial without role-specific exceptions.

Consequences: adapters must report observed capability; unknown dimensions deny. Reversible through a new schema version, not silent semantics. Validate: cross-harness expansion/narrowing matrix.

DEC-002 · Accepted

Separate proposal visibility from invocation authority

Addresses: REQ-002.

Options: keep zero pools; give orchestrator direct leaf calls; split proposable and invocable sets.

Evidence: current validator equates empty pools with safety. Rationale: planning quality needs lens visibility; graph safety needs explicit edge admission.

Consequences: catalog/render schema changes and negative tests. Easily reversed at catalog level. Validate: orchestrator sees lens metadata but cannot start a leaf.

DEC-003 · Accepted

Use one structured lifecycle adapter, never shell

Addresses: REQ-003.

Options: prose only; unrestricted Bash/run_command; typed local tool/MCP seam.

Evidence: current projections intentionally deny shell; official harness docs support tool allowlists/extensions. Rationale: typed operations enable lifecycle control without creating a command tunnel.

Consequences: installed CLI registration must be proven per harness. Adapter removal is reversible. Validate: allowlist, malformed input, arbitrary-command and non-orchestrator denial tests.

DEC-004 · Accepted

Exact route envelope on every edge

Addresses: REQ-004.

Options: parent prose; model-only field; exact provider/family/model/effort per dispatch.

Evidence: draft route has exact model/effort but no separate provider/family. Rationale: exact tuples prevent silent alias substitution and survive nested dispatch.

Consequences: live discovery and route digests are mandatory; unavailable work stops. Reversible by versioned migration. Validate: inherit/refine/reject/fail-closed fixtures.

DEC-005 · Accepted

Workflow evidence is a strict result envelope

Addresses: REQ-005.

Options: narrative return; generic event list; schema plus semantic reconciliation.

Evidence: current evidence summary is largely strings. Rationale: completion must be tied to dispatch identity, current state, required checks, and artifact digests.

Consequences: all workflows/adapters update together; older major versions reject. Validate: fixture corpus including forged, stale, incomplete, changed-state, and uncertain results.

DEC-006 · Accepted

Factory uses one recoverable transaction boundary

Addresses: REQ-006.

Options: direct multi-file writes; target-project-capable Factory; staged preimage transaction.

Evidence: Factory is already conceptually the sole fleet-definition writer. Rationale: generation and installation are one atomic user outcome.

Consequences: extra staging/rollback complexity and cold-reload proof; much smaller corruption risk. Validate: failure injection at render, promote, install, and reload steps.

DEC-007 · Accepted

Generation, cancellation, budgets, and ownership share one durable lifecycle

Addresses: REQ-007.

Options: independent in-memory checks; job-local cancellation; generation-fenced persistent supervisor.

Evidence: draft supervisor has jobs and concurrency but not a parent generation graph. Rationale: all four safeguards decide whether a process/result is still authorized.

Consequences: serialization and restart reconciliation become critical. Reversible via state migration/version. Validate: concurrent, crash/restart, cascade/drain, stale-event, lease/budget races.

DEC-008 · Accepted

Conformance is shared and benchmark-independent

Addresses: REQ-008.

Options: benchmark score as validation; adapter-specific tests; common provider-free contract suite.

Evidence: evaluation guidance already separates deterministic correctness from optional scoring. Rationale: control-plane correctness must not depend on data unavailable during real work.

Consequences: adapters implement a fixture driver and telemetry; LXC remains a later operational target. Validate: identical positive/negative fixture outcomes and backup rollback drill.

DEC-009 · Accepted

Default to two total verification passes

Addresses: REQ-009.

Options: single unchecked execution; unbounded repair; two total passes with one possible repair.

Evidence: repository repair policies cap repeat work; scorer is explicitly separate. Rationale: one independent check plus one focused correction balances assurance and cost while guaranteeing termination.

Consequences: complex failures may end exhausted/blocked/uncertain and need a new authorized goal. Config may lower the budget; increases require a reviewed cap. Validate: all five terminal outcomes, repeated-digest no-progress, and no-scorer assertions.

Delivery and rollout roadmap

Each stage leaves a coherent, testable state. Stage 7 is an operational gate requiring separate authorization; Stages 1–6 do not need LXC mutation.

Test and validation strategy

The verification authority is deliberately separate from mutation authority. Executor-owned unit/integration tests are necessary but not sufficient; Code Review/Coding Evaluation plus a read-only testing lens re-read the integrated state and validate the result envelope. The orchestrator alone reconciles both.

Test-data boundary

Fixtures are synthetic goals, roles, routes, repository paths/symbols, process trees, and deterministic command results. They contain no benchmark gold patch, hidden tests, historical attempts, score, issue-web lookup, or secrets. A benchmark task may have one attempt and emit one final patch; control-plane verification may inspect only the current checkout, task message, allowed deterministic test/compiler/linter output, and versioned workflow evidence. No scorer runs between cycles.

Risks, assumptions, and open questions

RSK-001 · High impact

Adapter authority drift

Likelihood: medium. Trigger: rendered allowlist differs from live tool inventory. Mitigation: observed-capability intersection and shared negative fixtures. Contingency: fail admission and restore prior projection. Owner: Agent Harness + Security lenses. Affects: REQ-001/003, DEC-001/003.

RSK-002 · Critical impact

Stale generation mutates after cancellation

Likelihood: medium. Trigger: descendant output after cancel/restart. Mitigation: durable fence, checks at mutation/result boundaries, cascade/drain. Contingency: reject/audit late event and block acceptance pending integrity check. Owner: Run-plane. Affects: REQ-007, DEC-007.

RSK-003 · High impact

Budget accounting races or unknown usage

Likelihood: medium. Trigger: concurrent child admission or provider omits token data. Mitigation: atomic reservations and conservative unknown accounting. Contingency: stop new admission as exhausted/blocked. Owner: Run-plane. Affects: REQ-004/007/009, DEC-007/009.

RSK-004 · High impact

Structurally valid but untruthful result

Likelihood: medium. Trigger: stale artifact/check or unsupported completion claim. Mitigation: semantic validation and independent current-state reconciliation. Contingency: uncertain disposition and targeted recheck. Owner: Orchestrator + independent verifier. Affects: REQ-005/009, DEC-005/009.

RSK-005 · Critical impact

Lifecycle adapter becomes a command tunnel

Likelihood: low-medium. Trigger: arbitrary argv/path/URL accepted. Mitigation: fixed operations, typed IDs/payloads, loopback/auth, no shell. Contingency: remove tool projection and revert to native-only control. Owner: Security + Agent Harness. Affects: REQ-003, DEC-003.

RSK-006 · High impact

Factory partial install or stale hot load

Likelihood: medium. Trigger: failure between catalog, projection, install, and session reload. Mitigation: one preimage transaction and cold-load proof. Contingency: exact rollback and fresh session. Owner: Factory. Affects: REQ-006, DEC-006.

RSK-007 · Medium impact

Symbol ownership is too coarse or too permissive

Likelihood: medium. Trigger: language parser cannot resolve a claim or nested symbols overlap. Mitigation: canonical grammar plus conservative path fallback. Contingency: serialize the broader file scope. Owner: Integration lens. Affects: REQ-007, DEC-007.

RSK-008 · Critical impact

LXC sync drifts settings or roles

Likelihood: medium. Trigger: model prefix, thinking/message value, adapter, or role checksum differs. Mitigation: fresh backup/settings manifest, staging, atomic promotion, offline conformance. Contingency: restore exact preimages; no benchmark/service continuation. Owner: Linux/Systemd + Integration lenses. Affects: REQ-008, DEC-008.

RSK-009 · Medium impact

Verification oscillates or hides uncertainty

Likelihood: medium. Trigger: repeated digest, contradictory review, missing proof at budget end. Mitigation: two-pass default, deterministic reconciliation, explicit uncertain evidence. Contingency: no-progress/exhausted/blocked and a new user-authorized goal. Owner: Orchestrator. Affects: REQ-009, DEC-009.

Assumptions

  • ASM-001: the dirty runplane/ work is intended as the supervisor base, but implementation must reconcile concurrent changes before editing.
  • ASM-002: exact structured-tool registration details can be implemented using the installed Claude and Antigravity extension mechanisms; ROAD-004 must verify actual installed-version capabilities and fail closed if unavailable.
  • ASM-003: language-qualified symbol ownership can fall back conservatively to file ownership when resolution is unavailable.
  • ASM-004: local/provider-free conformance can exercise all control-plane semantics without a model invocation.

Open questions that do not block Stage 2

  • OQ-001: Which bounded event/evidence retention period should be the operational default? Close with operator storage/support requirements before ROAD-003 rollout.
  • OQ-002: Should reviewed configuration permit more than two total verification passes, and what hard maximum? This design ships default 2 and may safely support only 1–2 until evidence justifies more.
  • OQ-003: What exact installed-version registration name will the structured lifecycle tool use in Claude and Antigravity? Close with ROAD-004 capability discovery; semantics and shell prohibition are already fixed.
  • OQ-004: Does LXC runtime require the provider prefix in the adapter model string or add it upstream? Close by snapshotting effective configuration before ROAD-007; preserve the user’s exact google/gemini-3.7-flash constraint either way.

Uninspected areas: live service state, process environment, effective auth/tool inventory, operational restart path, and benchmark behavior on LXC 135; provider billing/token telemetry edge cases; language-specific symbol extraction beyond the existing repository evidence. None authorizes guessing—each is a named delivery gate.

Final recommendation

Proceed with Option C: versioned shared contracts enforced at catalog/render, native dispatch, run-plane admission, result reconciliation, and adapter boundaries. Begin with ROAD-001’s schema/fixture slice because it gives every later stage a stable, reviewable target and is safe against the current dirty worktree. Then deliver authority/selection/route/result enforcement and generation lifecycle independently before joining them through the structured adapters.

Decision-ready conditions met: all nine requirements have observable acceptance criteria and trace through nine decisions, tests, risks, diagrams, and seven coherent stages; write and verification authority are separate; route and evidence contracts are separate; lifecycle safety is grouped under REQ-007; Factory and LXC operations are recoverable gates; the verification loop is bounded and scorer-free.

First coherent delivery slice: checked-in versioned schemas, semantic validators, and provider-free valid/invalid fixtures with no runtime switch. Conditions that change this design: an installed harness cannot expose a narrow structured tool, the existing run-plane cannot persist generation/ledger state safely, or exact route capabilities cannot be discovered. Any such condition triggers fail-closed adapter scope—not shell fallback or model substitution.

Measurable completion: shared cross-harness conformance passes; all authority expansion/direct-leaf/unavailable-route/stale-result cases reject; cancellation leaves zero current descendants/leases; ledger conservation holds; Factory and LXC rollback restore exact preimages; the two-pass loop reaches only its five terminal outcomes; and no validation consumes scorer/gold/hidden/history/issue-web data or paid models.

Handoff: use $anvil-implementation-plan to turn ROAD-001 through ROAD-007 into dependency-ordered tasks. Do not import tasks from Stage 1. ROAD-007 must remain explicitly gated on deployment/service authority and a fresh LXC snapshot.
